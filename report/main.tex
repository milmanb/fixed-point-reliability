\title{Stable but Wrong: Fixed-Point Residuals as\\Reference-Free Reliability Signals for Image Restoration}

\begin{abstract}
Idempotent networks are trained so that $f(f(y)) = f(y)$, and recent work reads a small
idempotence residual as a sign of a reliable prediction. We test it on small Fashion-MNIST
denoisers against the true per-image error. The residual is zero for any exact
projector, whatever the error, and a first-order expansion shows that it probes the Jacobian along
the model's own displacement. For learned models it ranks errors within a corruption level
(Spearman $\rho = 0.70$ for a bottleneck autoencoder, below its displacement's 0.77, and 0.86 with
skip connections), but each architecture has an unseen corruption under which its outputs are
stable but wrong and the residual misses them: pixel masks for the bottleneck model, large box
masks for the skip model. A conformal error bar built on the residual holds its nominal 90\%
coverage over all images but covers only 61\% and 74\% of pixel-mask images in the two models.
Cross-seed disagreement shares the blind spots. For the bottleneck model, per-level calibration lifts
the residual's pooled ranking from 0.55 to 0.89, but a level-mean predictor already reaches 0.77. A strong idempotence
penalty makes the residual a worse signal, and without a warm-up both models collapse to constant
images.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

Restoration networks run where the clean image is unknown, and a failed output can still look
plausible, so a signal that flags unreliable outputs without ground truth would be useful.

Idempotent Generative Networks (IGN)~\cite{shocher2024ign} train a network $f$ so that real data
are fixed points, $f(x) = x$, and other inputs land on that set, $f(f(y)) = f(y)$. Related
methods read the deviation from this property as a confidence
score~\cite{durasov2024zigzag,durasov2025it3,aljaff2025naign}. But an output that is a fixed point
need not be a correct one: an exact projector satisfies $P(P(y)) = P(y)$ for every input,
including inputs that it maps to a wrong image. We ask how the residual behaves for learned
models, which are only approximately idempotent, and how training for idempotence changes it, in
a controlled setting with known errors. We find that (i)~the residual is useful within a corruption level, but each of two
architectures has a corruption under which it misses the worst errors; (ii)~a strong idempotence
penalty makes it a worse signal, and without a warm-up it collapses the model to a constant image;
(iii)~as a conformal error bar it keeps its marginal guarantee but under-covers some unseen masks,
including its blind spot, which neither deeper iterates nor cross-seed disagreement
repair;
(iv)~brightness and level baselines are needed to read such signals correctly.

\paragraph{Related work.}
Idempotence has been used to train one-step generative
models~\cite{shocher2024ign,aljaff2025naign,zaman2025sign}, and NAIGN~\cite{aljaff2025naign} reads the drift of
a network as a distance to the data manifold. In supervised prediction,
ZigZag~\cite{durasov2024zigzag} reports per-sample correlations above 0.9 between a
self-consistency residual and the loss, while the $-0.94$ of IT$^3$~\cite{durasov2025it3} between
idempotence error and ImageNet-C accuracy is per inference batch. Closest to our setting, Huang et
al.~\cite{huang2023cycle} predict restoration uncertainty from forward-backward cycles, whose
features include $\norm{A_s f(y) - y}$; we test the residual itself and how idempotence training
changes it. We also use split conformal
prediction~\cite{angelopoulos2021gentle}, applied to imaging
in~\cite{teneggi2023krcps,everink2025sscp}, the excess area under the risk-coverage
curve~\cite{geifman2019bias}, and cross-model disagreement, proposed for language models to
complement self-consistency~\cite{hamidieh2026disagreement}.

\section{Method}

\paragraph{Setting.}
Let $x$ be a clean image and $y = A_s x + n$ an observation, with a known linear operator $A_s$
and Gaussian noise $n$; a model $f$ must estimate $x$ without seeing it. A \emph{level} is one
operator at one strength, a \emph{family} groups the levels of one operator, and we use 13 levels
in four families: Gaussian noise ($A_s = I$, $\sigma \in \{0.1, 0.2, 0.3, 0.5\}$), Gaussian blur
(standard deviation 0.5, 1, 1.5 or 2 pixels), pixel masks (each pixel zeroed with probability 0.25, 0.5 or
0.75) and box masks (one random square of side 8 or 14). Blur and masks add weak noise
($\sigma = 0.02$) before zeroing. Training sees Gaussian noise with $\sigma \le 0.25$ only, so
$\sigma \ge 0.3$, blur and masks are unseen.

\paragraph{Signals.}
With $|\Omega|$ the number of pixels, we compare
\begin{align*}
  g(y) &= \tfrac{1}{|\Omega|}\norm{f(f(y)) - f(y)}_1, & d(y) &= \tfrac{1}{|\Omega|}\norm{f(y) - y}_1,\\
  r_A(y) &= \tfrac{1}{|\Omega|}\norm{A_s f(y) - y}_1, & e(y) &= \tfrac{1}{|\Omega|}\norm{f(y) - x}_1.
\end{align*}
The residual $g$ and the displacement $d$ need only $f$ and $y$; the measurement residual $r_A$
also needs $A_s$ and equals $d$ for denoising. For Gaussian noise we add Stein's unbiased risk
estimate (SURE) of the squared error~\cite{stein1981sure,ramani2008mcsure}, with the divergence
from four Rademacher probes and finite differences ($\varepsilon = 10^{-3}$). Deeper iterates
$g_k = \tfrac{1}{|\Omega|}\norm{f^{k+1}(y) - f^k(y)}_1$ probe the orbit;
ensemble disagreement \mbox{$\mathrm{dis}(y) = \tfrac{1}{M|\Omega|}\sum_i \norm{f_i(y) - \bar f(y)}_1$}
over the $M = 3$ seeds of a setting, with $\bar f$ their mean, is an epistemic counterpart to
self-consistency.

\paragraph{Why the residual can be blind.}
Write $\delta(y) = f(y) - y$ for the displacement. A first-order expansion of the second
application of $f$ gives
\begin{equation}
  g(y) = \tfrac{1}{|\Omega|}\norm{f(y + \delta) - f(y)}_1
       \approx \tfrac{1}{|\Omega|}\norm{J_f(y)\,\delta(y)}_1,
  \label{eq:taylor}
\end{equation}
so the residual probes the Jacobian along the one direction in which the model has just moved.
For the nearest-point projection onto a smooth set, $\delta$ is normal to that set while $J_f$ projects onto
its tangent space, so $J_f\delta = 0$ whatever the error. A learned model can only be informative
where $J_f$ does not cancel its own displacement.

\paragraph{Models.}
The \emph{bottleneck} model is a convolutional autoencoder (602k parameters): three convolutions
(32, 64 and 64 channels, SiLU, stride 2 after the first) down to $7\times7$, a 64-dimensional bottleneck and a
mirrored decoder. The \emph{skip} model has the same convolutions with skip connections and no
bottleneck (230k parameters), so it can copy detail from its input. We train on 55{,}000
Fashion-MNIST~\cite{xiao2017fashion} images with $\sigma \sim \mathcal{U}[0.05, 0.25]$ for 15
epochs with Adam, minimizing
$\mathcal{L} = \norm{f(y) - x}_1 + \lid\,\norm{f(f(y)) - f(y)}_1$ averaged over pixels, with
$\lid \in \{0, 1\}$ and, for the bottleneck model, also 0.1; three seeds each. For $\lid > 0$ we
ramp $\lid$ linearly from 0 over five epochs (a warm-up); one run per model omits it. Gradients
normally flow through both applications of $f$; \emph{inner-only} training freezes the outer $f$
in $f(f(y))$, as in IGN, and \emph{outer-only} freezes the inner output. As exact references we
project onto a sphere around the training mean (radial) and onto the top 16, 64 or 256 principal
components (PCA).

\paragraph{Evaluation.}
On 10{,}000 test images we report Spearman's $\rho$ with $e$ within each level and pooled over
all levels (an unknown corruption), and the AUROC for the worst quartile of $e$. An image is \emph{flagged} when its signal is in the top quartile over all
levels, so a random score flags 25\% of any subset. Under masking the error grows with image
content, so we add brightness $b(y) = \operatorname{mean}(y)$ as a model-free baseline and report
partial $\rho$ given $b$. Calibration fits an isotonic regression of $e$ on a signal, in the
direction the data choose, per level or per family on half the images, scores the other half, and
is compared with each level's mean error. Bootstrap 95\% intervals are about $\pm 0.01$.

\subsection{Exact projectors}
For the radial and PCA projectors $g < 2\cdot10^{-15}$ while the mean error lies between 0.04
and 0.23: the residual cannot rank these errors, and deeper iterates only repeat it; $d$ still ranks them within a level ($\rho$ =
0.53 to 0.91).

\subsection{Learned models and their blind spots}
For the bottleneck model, $g$ ranks errors within a level with $\rho = 0.70$
(Table~\ref{tab:main}), below $d$ (0.77) and $r_A$ (0.80), and Eq.~\eqref{eq:taylor} holds in rank (0.81 to 0.97 between $g$ and its
linearization for $\sigma \le 0.3$). As the noise grows the residual holds up (0.75 at
$\sigma = 0.1$, 0.72 at 0.5) while $d$ falls from 0.93 to 0.28 and SURE from 0.91 to 0.40, though
against squared error SURE ranks best for $\sigma \le 0.3$. The skip model's residual ranks better
than the bottleneck's at 11 of the 13 levels (0.86; $d$ 0.49, $r_A$ 0.72), in partial $\rho$ too;
its median partial $\rho$ is lower (0.48 against 0.59) only because of the box masks. Brightness
alone reaches 0.51 to 0.55 within a level, and 0.96 under pixel masks, so raw scores under masks and
blur overstate the signals.

Pooled over all levels, $g$ reaches $\rho = 0.55$ and an AUROC of 0.69 for the bottleneck model,
against 0.12 for $d$ and 0.02 for $r_A$, whose scales differ between families; replacing $g$ by
its median per level still gives 0.46. The pooled score hides a blind spot
(Fig.~\ref{fig:failures}). Under 75\% pixel masks the bottleneck model's mean error is 0.234, five
times that at $\sigma = 0.1$, while its mean residual barely moves (0.014 against 0.011): it turns
sparse dots into a dim garment and treats it as a fixed point. Pixel masks hold 60\% of the
worst-quartile errors, and $g$ flags 18\% of them, fewer than a random score (Fig.~\ref{fig:blind}).
The skip model has the opposite blind spot: under box masks its within-level $\rho$ drops to 0.29
and 0.12 (bottleneck: 0.83 and 0.71), it flags under 1\% of the worst box-mask errors, and 83--87\%
of its stable-but-wrong images (top-10\% error, below-median $g$) are large boxes.

\subsection{Training for idempotence}
\label{sec:idem}
Without a warm-up, $\lid = 1$ collapses both architectures within one epoch to a constant image:
the bottleneck model to the per-pixel median training image (validation error 0.2106, residual
$10^{-6}$), the skip model to black (0.2885; residual $10^{-10}$), far above the $\lid = 0$
solutions (about 0.05): failures of optimization. At initialization the outputs vary across inputs
by $5\cdot10^{-6}$ (bottleneck) and $8\cdot10^{-3}$ (skip; clean images: 0.27), so a start as
constant as the bottleneck's is not required.

With the warm-up, mean error changes little (0.092 to 0.095; 0.086 to 0.084) but the residual
falls by 40\% and 61\% and becomes a worse signal: within-level $\rho$ falls from 0.70 to 0.62 and
from 0.86 to 0.63, with no overlap between seeds, and so do the noise, partial and pooled scores
and the pixel-mask flagged share; only the box-mask share rises. A weight of 0.1 leaves the
within-level $\rho$ unchanged (0.71). All three routings lower it (0.62; 0.61 inner-only; 0.65
outer-only). Only routes
reaching the outer application also lose partial $\rho$ and the pixel-mask share, lose more pooled
$\rho$ (0.06 and 0.05 against 0.02) and shrink $g$ more (40\% and 26\% against 6\%).

After per-level calibration, pooled $\rho$ is
0.89 for $g$, 0.93 for $d$ and 0.94 for $r_A$ in the bottleneck model and 0.92--0.93 in the skip
model, whose fits reverse $d$ and $r_A$ under noise, while the level-mean predictor
reaches 0.77 and 0.84; when only the family is
known, $g$ is the best calibrated signal in both (0.84 and 0.79).

Split conformal turns a signal into an upper bound on the error: an isotonic fit $\hat u$ of $e$ on the signal over a quarter of the test images, the
scores $e_i/\hat u(y_i)$ of a second quarter, their quantile $\hat q$ of rank
$\lceil (n{+}1)(1{-}\alpha)\rceil$ among these $n$, and $\hat e_{\mathrm{hi}} = \hat q\,\hat u$ on
the held-out half, which it covers with probability at least $1-\alpha$ under
exchangeability~\cite{angelopoulos2021gentle}. At $\alpha = 0.1$ the bound on $g$ meets its
nominal 90\% marginally. For the bottleneck model it is narrower than one that ignores the signal
(0.174 against 0.182), for the skip model slightly wider (0.173 against 0.170), and a level-mean
bound is narrower in both (0.138 and 0.129). By family it covers 0.99 to 1.00 of noise and blur,
but only 0.61 of pixel masks for the bottleneck model, and 0.74 of pixel and 0.76 of box masks for
the skip model, whose residual ranks pixel-mask errors well (Table~\ref{tab:coverage}). Calibrated
on the two training noise levels and deployed on the other eleven, coverage falls to 0.48 ($g$)
and 0.19 ($d$, $r_A$) for the bottleneck model and to 0.16 and 0.26 for the skip model.

Deeper iterates rank worse in three of the four settings of Table~\ref{tab:coverage} ($g_2$ 0.63
against 0.70 here), with unchanged coverage.
Ensemble disagreement ranks better within a level, except for the skip model at $\lid = 0$ (0.79
against 0.70 here; 0.84 against 0.86 there), and loses less under the penalty, but it misses the same families
(Fig.~\ref{fig:blind}) and its bound covers 0.59 of pixel masks here: three seeds of one architecture
fail the same way on an unseen operator. Rejecting the 20\% of images with the
largest $g$ lowers mean error from 0.092 to 0.084 (oracle 0.064); rejecting by $d$, $r_A$ or
brightness does not (0.092, 0.100, 0.097). By nAURC, the excess area under the risk-coverage
curve~\cite{geifman2019bias} scaled as in Table~\ref{tab:coverage}, $g$ beats disagreement in three
of four settings (0.41 against 0.48 here).

\section{Discussion}
A small idempotence residual is weak evidence of a correct restoration: for a projector it carries
none, and for the learned models it is useful within a level but misses an unseen corruption whose
outputs are stable and wrong. Which corruption this is depends on the architecture, so a residual
validated on some corruptions need not transfer. As an error bar this becomes a cost: the marginal
guarantee holds while coverage on some unseen masks, including the model's blind spot, fails
silently.

With $\lid = 1$ the residual became a worse signal while restoration held. A diagnostic that is
also optimized as a loss can lose information; here the loss in brightness-controlled ranking,
and most of that in pooled ranking, needed gradients through the outer application, and methods
that minimize the residual at
test time~\cite{durasov2025it3} may face the same issue, which we did not test. Cross-seed
disagreement ranks better for penalized models, at the cost of three trainings, but agrees with
itself where it is wrong. Neither $d$ nor $r_A$ is safer: within a level they rank the bottleneck
model's errors better than $g$, the skip model's worse. No signal compares
corruptions without calibration, and a level-mean predictor already reaches most of the calibrated
performance.

\paragraph{Limitations and deviations from the proposal.}
Coverage is marginal and per family, not per pixel. Beyond the proposal we added box masks, SURE, the baselines, the second architecture, the
ablations, conformal bounds, selective risk, deeper iterates and disagreement, and $\lid > 0$
needed a warm-up. The evidence is one
small dataset, two small models, three seeds per setting and one full collapse run per
architecture (two shorter bottleneck runs also collapsed). Fashion-MNIST's black background makes
brightness a strong baseline.
