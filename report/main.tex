\begin{abstract}
A learned restoration model does not signal when its output is wrong. Idempotent networks are
trained so that $f(f(y)) = f(y)$, and recent work reads a small idempotence residual as a sign of
a reliable prediction. We test this reading on Fashion-MNIST denoisers under noise, blur and
masking, against the true per-image error. A first-order expansion shows that the residual probes
the Jacobian along the model's own displacement, so it is exactly zero for projectors. For
learned models it ranks errors within a corruption (Spearman $\rho = 0.70$ for a bottleneck
autoencoder, 0.86 with skip connections), but the bottleneck model misses its largest failures:
under pixel masks it returns dim, plausible images that are near fixed points. Training for
idempotence makes the residual a worse signal in both architectures ($\rho$ falls to 0.62 and
0.63), and without a warm-up it collapses both models to constant images. Per-operator calibration lifts the pooled ranking of the residual from 0.55 to 0.89, but
a baseline that knows only the corruption level already reaches 0.77.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

Restoration networks are often applied where the clean image is unknown. When they fail, the
output can still look plausible, so a signal that flags unreliable outputs without ground truth
would be useful.

Idempotent Generative Networks (IGN)~\cite{shocher2024ign} train a network $f$ so that real data
are fixed points, $f(x) = x$, and other inputs land on that set, $f(f(y)) = f(y)$. Related
methods use the deviation from this property as a confidence score. ZigZag~\cite{durasov2024zigzag}
and IT$^3$~\cite{durasov2025it3} report that a self-consistency residual tracks prediction
quality, and NAIGN~\cite{aljaff2025naign} reads the drift of a network as a distance to the data
manifold. It is tempting to conclude that an output which is a fixed point is also a correct one.

This conclusion does not hold in general. An exact projector satisfies $P(P(y)) = P(y)$ for every
input, including inputs that it maps to a wrong image. The open questions are how the residual
behaves for learned models, which are only approximately idempotent, and whether training for
idempotence changes that. We study them in a small, controlled setting with known corruptions,
where the true error is available offline. Our contributions are:
(i)~an evaluation protocol with brightness and severity baselines and a per-operator calibration;
(ii)~an analysis of the residual as a Jacobian probe, tested on two architectures that differ in
how close they are to a projector;
(iii)~an ablation of idempotence training over its weight, gradient routing and architecture.

\paragraph{Related work.}
Idempotence has been used to train one-step generative
models~\cite{shocher2024ign,aljaff2025naign} and to distill diffusion
models~\cite{zaman2025sign}. In supervised prediction, self-consistency residuals serve as
uncertainty estimates. ZigZag~\cite{durasov2024zigzag} reports per-sample correlations with the
loss above 0.9, while the $-0.94$ that IT$^3$~\cite{durasov2025it3} reports between idempotence
error and accuracy on ImageNet-C is computed per inference batch. Closest to our setting, Huang et
al.~\cite{huang2023cycle} predict the uncertainty of deblurring and super-resolution networks from
forward-backward cycles, whose features include $\norm{A_s f(y) - y}$. For denoising, SURE and
its Monte-Carlo form estimate the error without references~\cite{stein1981sure,ramani2008mcsure},
and the posterior covariance of a minimum mean squared error denoiser is its Jacobian times the
noise variance~\cite{manor2024posterior}. Data consistency cannot detect errors in the null space
of the operator~\cite{bhadra2021hallucinations}, and perfect perceptual quality costs
distortion~\cite{blau2018perception}.

\section{Method}

\paragraph{Setting.}
Let $x$ be a clean image and $y = A_s x + n$ an observation, with a known linear operator $A_s$
and Gaussian noise $n$, where $s$ indexes the corruption. A model $f$ returns an estimate $f(y)$,
and $x$ is not available at test time. We use four corruption families with 13 levels: Gaussian
noise ($A_s = I$, $\sigma \in \{0.1, 0.2, 0.3, 0.5\}$), Gaussian blur (width 0.5, 1, 1.5 or 2
pixels), pixel masks (each pixel zeroed with probability 0.25, 0.5 or 0.75) and box masks (one
random square of side 8 or 14). Blur and masks add weak noise ($\sigma = 0.02$), which the masks
apply before zeroing.

\paragraph{Signals.}
With $|\Omega|$ the number of pixels, we compare
\begin{align*}
  g(y) &= \tfrac{1}{|\Omega|}\norm{f(f(y)) - f(y)}_1, & d(y) &= \tfrac{1}{|\Omega|}\norm{f(y) - y}_1,\\
  r_A(y) &= \tfrac{1}{|\Omega|}\norm{A_s f(y) - y}_1, & e(y) &= \tfrac{1}{|\Omega|}\norm{f(y) - x}_1.
\end{align*}
The idempotence residual $g$ and the displacement $d$ need only $f$ and $y$. The measurement
residual $r_A$ also needs $A_s$, and equals $d$ for denoising. The error $e$ is used only for
evaluation. For Gaussian noise we also compute Stein's unbiased estimate of the mean squared
error~\cite{stein1981sure},
$\mathrm{SURE}(y) = \tfrac{1}{|\Omega|}\norm{f(y)-y}_2^2 - \sigma^2
+ \tfrac{2\sigma^2}{|\Omega|}\operatorname{div} f(y)$, with the divergence from four Rademacher
probes and finite differences ($\varepsilon = 10^{-3}$), a variant of~\cite{ramani2008mcsure}.

\paragraph{Why the residual can be blind.}
Write $\delta(y) = f(y) - y$ for the displacement. A first-order expansion of the second
application of $f$ gives
\begin{equation}
  g(y) = \tfrac{1}{|\Omega|}\norm{f(y + \delta) - f(y)}_1
       \approx \tfrac{1}{|\Omega|}\norm{J_f(y)\,\delta(y)}_1,
  \label{eq:taylor}
\end{equation}
so the residual probes the Jacobian along the one direction in which the model has just moved.
For an exact projector onto a smooth set, $\delta$ is normal to that set while $J_f$ projects onto
its tangent space, so $J_f\delta = 0$ and the residual vanishes whatever the error. Our projectors
show exactly this: $g < 2\cdot10^{-15}$ and the right side of Eq.~\eqref{eq:taylor} is $10^{-13}$,
the size of the finite-difference round-off. For a trained network the two sides agree in rank
(Spearman 0.89). The expansion predicts that a model further from a projector keeps more of the
error in $J_f\delta$, so its residual should be more informative.

\paragraph{Models.}
The \emph{bottleneck} model is a convolutional autoencoder with 602k parameters: three
convolutions (base width 32, SiLU, stride 2 after the first) down to $7\times7$, a 64-dimensional
bottleneck, a mirrored decoder and a sigmoid output. The \emph{skip} model has the same
convolutions with skip connections and no bottleneck (230k parameters). It can copy detail from
its input, so it is further from a projector. Neither uses normalization layers. We train on
55{,}000 Fashion-MNIST~\cite{xiao2017fashion} images with Gaussian noise,
$\sigma \sim \mathcal{U}[0.05, 0.25]$, for 15 epochs with Adam (learning rate $10^{-3}$, cosine
decay, batch 256), and minimize
\begin{equation}
  \mathcal{L} = \norm{f(y) - x}_1 + \lid\,\norm{f(f(y)) - f(y)}_1,
  \label{eq:loss}
\end{equation}
averaged over pixels, with gradients through both applications of $f$. Both models are trained
with $\lid \in \{0, 1\}$, and the bottleneck model also with $\lid = 0.1$ and with updates to the
inner application only, as in IGN; three seeds each. For $\lid > 0$ the weight is ramped from 0
over five epochs; one run per model omits the ramp. As exact references we project onto a sphere
around the training mean (radial) and onto the top-$k$ principal subspace (PCA-$k$).

\paragraph{Evaluation.}
On the 10{,}000 test images we report Spearman's $\rho$ between a signal and $e$, within each
corruption level and pooled over all 13 levels, which corresponds to an unknown corruption. We
call an image \emph{flagged} when its signal is in the top quartile. Under masking the error grows
with the amount of content in an image, so we add the brightness $b(y) = \operatorname{mean}(y)$ as
a model-free baseline and report partial correlations given $b$. A severity-only baseline replaces
a signal by its median over the level. Bootstrap intervals (1000 resamples of test images) have a
half-width of about 0.01. For calibration we fit isotonic regression of $e$ on a signal, per
level or per family of levels, on half of the test images and score the other half.

\subsection{Exact projectors have zero residual}
For the radial and PCA projectors $g < 2\cdot10^{-15}$ everywhere, while the mean error lies
between 0.04 and 0.23, so it cannot rank images. The displacement ranks errors
well when the fixed-point set is tight (PCA-16, median $\rho = 0.91$) and can reverse when it is
not (radial at $\sigma = 0.2$, $\rho = -0.46$).

\subsection{The residual depends on the model}
For the bottleneck model, $g$ ranks errors within a level with $\rho = 0.70$ (Table~\ref{tab:main}).
Under noise it holds up as the noise grows (0.75 at $\sigma = 0.1$, 0.72 at 0.5), while $d$ falls
from 0.93 to 0.28 and SURE from 0.91 to 0.40. As Eq.~\eqref{eq:taylor} predicts, the skip model's
residual ranks errors better (0.86; 0.92 under noise). Its displacement is worse (0.49) and under
noise even negative ($-0.62$): the model moves least on the images where it leaves noise in place.
Raw within-level scores overstate every signal: brightness alone reaches a median $\rho$ of 0.51,
and 0.96 under pixel masks. Controlling for it reverses the order of the architectures (partial
$\rho$ 0.59 and 0.48).

Pooled over all levels, $g$ reaches $\rho = 0.55$ for the bottleneck model, against 0.12 for $d$
and 0.02 for $r_A$, whose scales differ between corruptions. This hides a systematic failure
(Fig.~\ref{fig:failures}). Under pixel masks with 75\% dropped, the
bottleneck model's mean error is 0.234, five times that at $\sigma = 0.1$, while its mean residual
barely moves (0.014 against 0.011): it turns sparse dots into a dim garment and treats it as a
fixed point. Pixel masks hold 60\% of the worst-quartile errors, and $g$ flags 18\% of them. The
skip model's residual flags 68\%.

\subsection{Training for idempotence}
\label{sec:idem}
Without a warm-up, $\lid = 1$ collapses both architectures within one epoch to a constant, exactly
idempotent image: the per-pixel median training image for the bottleneck model (validation error
0.21059 for both) and a black image for the skip model (0.287, the mean pixel value). The loss
there, about 0.21, is far above its value at the $\lid = 0$ solution (about 0.06), so this is a
failure of optimization. The bottleneck model starts almost constant (output spread
$5\cdot10^{-6}$ across inputs) and the skip model does not ($8\cdot10^{-3}$), so a near-constant
start decides where a model collapses, not whether.

With the ramp, training succeeds and restoration barely changes, but the residual shrinks and
becomes a worse signal in both architectures: within-level $\rho$ falls from 0.70 to 0.62 and
from 0.86 to 0.63, with no overlap between seeds, and the share of the worst pixel-mask errors
that $g$ flags from 18\% to 10\% and from 68\% to 31\%. A weight of 0.1 leaves the ranking
unchanged (0.71). Updating only the inner application, as in IGN, preserves most of the signal
(partial $\rho$ 0.61; 22\% flagged). Since the full gradient is the sum of the inner and outer
ones, the damage comes mainly from the outer route, which reshapes $f$ so that its current outputs
become fixed points.

\subsection{Calibration}
Pooled scores are low because each signal has a different scale under each corruption. Calibrated
per level, pooled $\rho$ rises to 0.89 for $g$, 0.93 for $d$ and 0.94 for $r_A$ (bottleneck model)
and to 0.92--0.93 for the skip model. A prediction that knows only the level's mean error already
reaches 0.77 and 0.84, so the per-image signals add 0.08 to 0.17. When only the corruption family
is known, $g$ is the best calibrated signal for the bottleneck model (0.84, against 0.79 for $d$
and 0.77 for $r_A$).

\section{Discussion}
A small idempotence residual is weak evidence of a correct restoration. For a projector it carries
no information, because the Jacobian removes the displacement it probes. Further from that limit
the residual helps more: the skip model's residual ranks errors better under noise and sees the
pixel-mask failures that the bottleneck model misses.

Training for idempotence makes the residual a worse signal, in both architectures and more with a
larger weight: a quantity optimized as a loss stops being a reliable diagnostic, most of all when
the map is reshaped around its own outputs. This is a caution for methods that minimize the
residual at test time~\cite{durasov2025it3}.

With a known operator the measurement residual is the stronger signal, and every signal needs
per-operator calibration before it can compare corruptions. Baselines matter as much as signals:
brightness explains much of the within-level ranking under masks, and a severity-only prediction
reaches most of the calibrated performance.

\paragraph{Limitations and deviations from the proposal.}
We proposed to calibrate the signals; we calibrate their ranking, not a coverage guarantee. We
added box masks, SURE, the baselines, the second architecture and the ablations, and $\lid > 0$
needed a warm-up. We use one small dataset and synthetic corruptions, and train on noise only. The
architectures also differ in size, Fashion-MNIST's black background makes brightness a strong
baseline, and the role of the outer route is inferred rather than tested directly.
