\begin{abstract}
Idempotent networks are trained so that $f(f(y)) = f(y)$, and recent work reads a small
fixed-point residual as a sign of a reliable output. We test this reading on two small
Fashion-MNIST denoisers, trained on Gaussian noise and evaluated on 13 corruption levels with known
per-image errors. The residual is zero for any exact projector, whatever its error, and to first
order it only measures the Jacobian along the model's own step. First, for learned
models it ranks errors within a corruption level, but each architecture has an unseen corruption
whose wrong outputs are stable: pixel masks for a bottleneck autoencoder and box masks for a
network with skip connections. There the residual flags fewer of the worst errors than a random
score, a conformal error bar built on it covers 61\% and 76\% of the images against a nominal
90\%, and disagreement between seeds misses the same corruptions. The displacement and the
measurement residual have their own blind spots. For two pretrained standard denoisers on natural
images the residual ranks errors within a level even less well. Second, a strong idempotence penalty
barely changes the error but makes the residual a worse signal. Third, each level's mean error
alone gives most of the pooled $\rho$ of calibrated signals. A small fixed-point residual is thus
weak evidence of a correct restoration.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

Restoration networks run where the clean image is unknown, and a wrong output can look plausible
(Fig.~\ref{fig:concept}b). A signal that flags such outputs without ground truth would be useful.

Idempotent Generative Networks (IGN)~\cite{shocher2024ign} train a network $f$ so that real data
are fixed points, $f(x) = x$, and other inputs land on that set, $f(f(y)) = f(y)$. Related methods
use deviations from these properties, such as the fixed-point residual $g$, as confidence
scores~\cite{durasov2024zigzag,durasov2025it3,aljaff2025naign}. A fixed point need not be correct,
however. An exact projector satisfies $P(P(y)) = P(y)$ for every input, including inputs that it
maps to a wrong image (Fig.~\ref{fig:concept}a), and overparameterized autoencoders store training
images as attractors~\cite{radhakrishnan2020memory}. We ask whether a small residual means a small
error for learned models, which are only approximately idempotent, and how idempotence training
changes this. In a controlled setting where every output's error is known, we
find three things.
(i)~The residual ranks errors within a corruption level, but it misses the worst errors of an
unseen corruption in each of our two architectures (Fig.~\ref{fig:concept}b), as do conformal
error bars built on it and disagreement between seeds. For two pretrained standard denoisers on
natural images the residual ranks errors even less well.
(ii)~A strong idempotence penalty makes the residual a worse signal and, without a warm-up,
collapses the model to a constant image.
(iii)~Such signals need baselines: brightness alone ranks pixel-mask errors well, and each level's
mean error gives most of the calibrated ranking.

\paragraph{Related work.}
Idempotence is used to train one-step generative
models~\cite{shocher2024ign,aljaff2025naign,zaman2025sign}, and NAIGN~\cite{aljaff2025naign} uses
a network's drift as a distance to the data manifold. In supervised tasks,
ZigZag~\cite{durasov2024zigzag} reports per-sample correlations above 0.9 between a
self-consistency residual and the loss, and IT$^3$~\cite{durasov2025it3} a correlation of $-0.94$
between idempotence error and \mbox{ImageNet-C} accuracy per inference batch. Closest to our work, Huang
et~al.~\cite{huang2023cycle} estimate uncertainty from forward-backward cycles, whose differences
are our $g$ and its iterates for denoising, and from $\norm{A_s f(y) - y}$. We score the residual
against each image's known error, per corruption family, and test how idempotence training
changes it. We use split conformal prediction~\cite{angelopoulos2021gentle}, as in
imaging~\cite{teneggi2023krcps,everink2025sscp}, and cross-model disagreement, proposed for
language models to complement self-consistency~\cite{hamidieh2026disagreement}.

\section{Method}

\paragraph{Setting.}
Let $x$ be a clean image and $y = A_s x + n$ an observation, with a known linear operator $A_s$ and
Gaussian noise $n$. A model $f$ estimates $x$ from $y$ alone. A \emph{level} is one operator at one
strength, and a \emph{family} groups the levels of one operator. We use 13 levels in four families:
Gaussian noise ($A_s = I$, $\sigma \in \{0.1, 0.2, 0.3, 0.5\}$), Gaussian blur (standard deviation
0.5, 1, 1.5 or 2 pixels), pixel masks (each pixel zeroed with probability 0.25, 0.5 or 0.75) and
box masks (a random square of side 8 or 14). Blur and masks add weak noise ($\sigma = 0.02$, before
the mask). Training sees Gaussian noise with $\sigma \le 0.25$ only, and $\sigma \ge 0.3$, blur and
masks are therefore unseen.

\paragraph{Signals.}
With $|\Omega|$ the number of pixels, we compare
\begin{align*}
  g(y) &= \tfrac{1}{|\Omega|}\norm{f(f(y)) - f(y)}_1, & d(y) &= \tfrac{1}{|\Omega|}\norm{f(y) - y}_1,\\
  r_A(y) &= \tfrac{1}{|\Omega|}\norm{A_s f(y) - y}_1, & e(y) &= \tfrac{1}{|\Omega|}\norm{f(y) - x}_1,
\end{align*}
where the residual $g$ and the displacement $d$ need only $f$ and $y$, and the measurement
residual $r_A$ also needs $A_s$ (for denoising $r_A = d$). We also test brightness
$b(y) = \operatorname{mean}(y)$ as a model-free baseline, the deeper iterates
$g_k$ (as $g$, between $f^{k+1}(y)$ and $f^k(y)$), Stein's unbiased risk estimate (SURE) of
the squared error~\cite{stein1981sure,ramani2008mcsure}, and the disagreement
$\mathrm{dis}$ between the three seeds (mean absolute deviation from their mean).

\paragraph{Why the residual can be blind.}
Write $\delta(y) = f(y) - y$ for the displacement. A first-order expansion of the second
application of $f$ gives
\begin{equation}
  g(y) = \tfrac{1}{|\Omega|}\norm{f(y + \delta) - f(y)}_1
       \approx \tfrac{1}{|\Omega|}\norm{J_f(y)\,\delta(y)}_1,
  \label{eq:taylor}
\end{equation}
and the residual thus probes the Jacobian $J_f$ along the model's own step. For the nearest-point
projection onto a smooth set (within its reach), $\delta$ is normal to the set and $J_f$
annihilates the normal directions. Hence $J_f\delta = 0$ whatever the error
(Fig.~\ref{fig:concept}a). To first order, a learned model is informative only where $J_f$ does
not cancel $\delta$. For the bottleneck model under noise with $\sigma \le 0.3$, Eq.~\eqref{eq:taylor}
holds in rank ($\rho = 0.81$ to 0.97 between $g$ and its linearization), but not reliably at
$\sigma = 0.5$ (0.20 to 0.81).

\paragraph{Models.}
The \emph{bottleneck} model is a convolutional autoencoder (602k parameters) with three
convolutions (32, 64 and 64 channels) down to $7\times7$ and a 64-dimensional bottleneck. The
\emph{skip} model has the same convolutions with skip connections and no bottleneck (230k
parameters), and it can copy detail from its input. We train on 55{,}000
Fashion-MNIST~\cite{xiao2017fashion} images with $\sigma \sim \mathcal{U}[0.05, 0.25]$ for 15
epochs with Adam, minimizing $\mathcal{L} = \norm{f(y) - x}_1 + \lid\,\norm{f(f(y)) - f(y)}_1$
averaged over pixels, with $\lid \in \{0, 1\}$ and three seeds each. For $\lid > 0$ we ramp $\lid$
up from 0 over five epochs (a warm-up). For the bottleneck model we also try $\lid = 0.1$ and two
routings of the penalty's gradient: through the inner application of $f$ only, as in IGN, or the
outer one only. As exact references we project onto a sphere around the training mean
(radial) and onto the top 16, 64 or 256 principal components (PCA).

\paragraph{Evaluation.}
On 10{,}000 test images we compute Spearman's $\rho$ of each signal with $e$ within each level
(median over levels per seed, mean over seeds) and pooled over all levels, as for an
unknown corruption, and the AUROC for detecting the worst quartile of $e$. An image is
\emph{flagged} when its signal is in the top quartile over all levels, which a random score does
for 25\% of any subset. Under masks the error grows with the image content, and we
therefore report partial $\rho$ given $b$. Per-level bootstrap 95\% intervals over test images are
about $\pm 0.01$ to $\pm 0.02$. Calibration fits a monotone map from a signal to $e$ per level on
half of the images and scores the rest. Split conformal prediction~\cite{angelopoulos2021gentle}
turns a signal into an upper bound $\hat e_{\mathrm{hi}}$ on $e$. An isotonic fit of $e$ on the
signal (a quarter of the test images) is scaled by the conformal quantile of the $n$ ratios of $e$
to the fit on a second quarter. The bound covers $e$ on the held-out half with probability at least
$1-\alpha-12/n$ for exchangeable images of 13 rows each (adapting~\cite{angelopoulos2021gentle}).
When two models are compared, the bottleneck model and DnCNN-B come first.

\section{Results}


\subsection{Exact projectors are blind}
For the radial and PCA projectors $g < 2\cdot10^{-15}$, but their mean error lies between 0.04
and 0.23. The displacement $d$ still ranks their errors within most
levels (median $\rho = 0.53$ to 0.91).

\subsection{Within a level the residual ranks errors}
For the learned models $g$ ranks errors within a level (Table~\ref{tab:main}): $\rho = 0.70$ for
the bottleneck model, below $d$ and $r_A$, and 0.86 for the skip model, well above them. As the
noise grows from $\sigma = 0.1$ to 0.5, the bottleneck model's $g$ keeps its ranking (0.75 to 0.72),
but $d$ falls from 0.93 to 0.28 and SURE from 0.91 to 0.40. Brightness alone reaches $\rho = 0.51$ to 0.55
within a level and 0.87 to 0.97 under pixel masks, and raw scores under masks and blur thus
overstate the signals.

Across levels the residual mostly detects the level. Pooled over all levels, $g$ reaches
$\rho = 0.55$ for the bottleneck model, against 0.12 for $d$ and 0.02 for $r_A$, whose scales
differ between families, and replacing $g$ by its median per level still gives 0.46. Calibrated per
level, all three signals reach a pooled $\rho$ of 0.89 to 0.94 in both models, but a predictor that
knows only the level's mean error already reaches 0.77 and 0.84.

\subsection{Each architecture has a blind spot}
The pooled score hides a blind spot in each model (Figs.~\ref{fig:concept}b and~\ref{fig:blind}).
Pixel masks hold 60\% of the bottleneck model's worst-quartile errors, and $g$ flags 18\% of them,
fewer than a random score. Under 75\% pixel masks the mean error is 5.4 times that at
$\sigma = 0.1$, but the mean residual rises only from 0.011 to 0.014: the model turns sparse dots
into a dim garment and treats it as a fixed point. The skip model's $g$ flags 68\% of its worst
pixel-mask errors, but the model fails on box masks: it leaves the box empty, and a second pass
keeps it empty. For boxes of side 8 and 14 its within-level $\rho$ is 0.29 and 0.12, $g$ flags
under 1\% of the worst errors, and 83--87\% of its stable-but-wrong images (Fig.~\ref{fig:concept}b)
are boxes of side 14.

The conformal bound on $g$ falls short on both blind spots and one more family. At
$\alpha = 0.1$ it covers 90\% of all images and 99 to 100\% of noise and blur, but only 61\% of
the bottleneck's pixel-mask images, 76\% of the skip model's box-mask images, and 74\% of its
pixel-mask images, whose worst errors its residual flags well. It is about as wide as a bound without
the signal, and a bound on the level mean is narrower. Calibrated on two in-range noise
levels, it covers only 48\% and 16\% of the other eleven. Over the four settings with
$\lid \in \{0, 1\}$, disagreement between seeds ranks better than $g$ within a level in
three, but it misses the same families (Fig.~\ref{fig:blind}), and its bound covers 59\% of the
bottleneck's pixel-mask images. Deeper iterates $g_k$ rank worse in three settings.

\subsection{Idempotence training weakens the residual}
\label{sec:idem}
Without a warm-up (one run per architecture), $\lid = 1$ collapses both models within one epoch to
a constant map with zero residual, the per-pixel median training image and black. This is a
failure of optimization: at $\sigma = 0.2$ their validation errors are 0.21 and 0.29, against 0.05
and 0.04 for $\lid = 1$ with the warm-up. With the warm-up, the mean error changes little
(0.092 to 0.095 and 0.086 to 0.084), but the residual falls by 40\% and 61\% and becomes a worse
signal. Within-level $\rho$ falls from 0.70 to 0.62 and from 0.86 to 0.63, with no overlap between
seeds, and the noise, partial and pooled scores fall too (Table~\ref{tab:main}). A weight of 0.1
leaves the within-level $\rho$ unchanged (0.71), and routing the penalty's gradient through the
inner or the outer application only lowers it as well (0.61 and 0.65).

\subsection{Two standard denoisers on natural images}
\label{sec:standard}
We ran two pretrained blind Gaussian denoisers trained on natural images with noise up to
$\sigma = 55/255$ and $50/255$, \mbox{DnCNN-B}~\cite{zhang2017dncnn} (0.67M parameters) and
Restormer~\cite{zamir2022restormer} (26M), on 408 crops ($128\times128$) of the 68 BSD68
images~\cite{martin2001bsd}, held out from their training. Both reproduce their published BSD68
PSNR within 0.01 dB. We use the 13 levels of Sec.~2, with boxes of side 37 and 64 and blur
reflected at the crop border, and clip outputs to $[0, 1]$, as when they are saved. Within a level
their residual ranks errors less well than for our models (median $\rho = 0.45$ and 0.19, or 0.43
and 0.11 without the clip, with per-level intervals of about $\pm 0.12$ when resampling source
images). Both leave the blur in place: their error is
78--91\% of the input's, and from width 0.5 to 2 it grows 2.7-fold while $g$ falls by 25\% and
11\%. Box masks hold 8\% and 16\% of their worst errors, and $g$ flags 1\% of them
(Fig.~\ref{fig:blind}). For Restormer this needs the clip: its output dips below zero inside the
box, the clip sets those values to zero on both passes, and without it $g$ flags 94\% of these errors.
Pooled, $g$ reaches $\rho = 0.69$ and 0.75, but its level medians alone reach 0.74 and 0.71.

\section{Discussion}
A small fixed-point residual is weak evidence of a correct restoration. For a projector it carries
none, and for our learned models it ranks errors within a level but misses an unseen corruption
whose outputs are stable and wrong. Which corruption this is depends on the architecture, and a
residual checked on some corruptions can fail on others. Conformal error bars inherit the blind
spots: the 90\% guarantee holds over all images, but coverage on some unseen masks falls to 61 to
76\% without warning, and a bound that covers every family must be sized for the worst one. Fixed
points also arise for reasons unrelated to the error: clipping the output to the image range makes
Restormer's output nearly fixed inside the box.

Neither $d$ nor $r_A$ is safer: averaged over seeds, they flag at most 2\% of the worst blur and
box-mask errors of each model in Table~\ref{tab:main}. With $\lid = 1$ the residual shrank by 40\%
and 61\% and ranked the errors worse while restoration held. Methods that minimize it at test
time~\cite{durasov2025it3} may face this issue too.

\paragraph{Limitations and deviations from the proposal.}
Our models were trained on one small dataset, with three seeds per setting and one full collapse
run per architecture. The standard denoisers were only evaluated, on 68 natural images, and their
comparison with our models mixes model and data. No IGN, ZigZag or IT$^3$ model was tested. The
conformal bound covers an image's mean error, not each pixel. Beyond the
proposal we added box masks, SURE, baselines, the skip model, ablations, conformal bounds,
deeper iterates, disagreement and the standard denoisers, and $\lid > 0$ needed a
warm-up.
